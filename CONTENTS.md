## 5. Research Questions and Validation

The series investigates five broad questions:

1. **Coverage:** When is language an adequate interface but an inadequate computational substrate?
2. **Representation:** Which tasks benefit measurably from native graphs, formal languages, solvers, or other structures after translation costs?
3. **Economics:** When do structural specialization and routing outweigh the fixed and ongoing costs of a hybrid system?
4. **Sovereignty:** How can independent actors share intelligence capabilities without surrendering ownership, policy authority, or private context?
5. **Growth:** Which new structures should be promoted, rejected, revised, or retired on the basis of evidence and governance?

### Minimum Evaluation Principles

- Compare against strong **LLM-only**, **LLM-with-tools**, and relevant **specialist/hybrid** baselines.
- Report end-to-end quality, latency, compute and engineering cost, verification effort, security, and maintenance burden.
- Count localization errors, representation translation losses, and policy violations—not only successful executions.
- Include counterexamples where a simpler generalist solution wins.
- Separate conceptual definitions, design proposals, testable hypotheses, and empirically supported findings.

## 9. Closing Orientation

BLB-SHI does not argue that natural language or LLMs are unimportant, nor that specialization always wins. It asks how to identify where a language-centered model is sufficient, where a different representation or execution substrate is warranted, and how those components can coexist under verifiable contracts, sovereign ownership, and accountable governance.

**Core trajectory:** Language Intelligence → Representation-Aware Specialization → Boundary-Aware Hybrid Architecture → Evidence-Gated Structural Growth.
