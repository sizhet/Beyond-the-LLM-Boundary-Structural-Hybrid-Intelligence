Beyond the LLM Boundary: Structural Hybrid Intelligence, Representation Barriers, and the Future of AI Architecture (BLB-SHI, v1.0.0) is an eight-paper research collection by Sizhe Tan & GPT-Obot examining where language-centric large language models provide a powerful general interface but may not offer the most suitable internal representation, execution method, ownership model, or governance structure for every task. The collection develops Computational Representation Barriers, the economics of scaling versus structural specialization, Perspective Sovereignty, Localization–Execution Decoupling, CCC-native structural semantic units, and BASHI (Boundary-Aware Structural Hybrid Intelligence), a proposed architecture coordinating structural localization, specialized per-node execution, evidence and counter-evidence verification, policy controls, and governed structural growth. These are conceptual and architectural research proposals, not claims of empirically demonstrated superiority; the papers outline falsifiable hypotheses and comparative evaluation against LLM-only, LLM-with-tools, and conventional hybrid baselines.
