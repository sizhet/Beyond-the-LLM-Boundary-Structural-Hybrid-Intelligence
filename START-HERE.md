**BLB-SHI · v1.0.0 · October 2026**  
**Authors:** Sizhe Tan & GPT-Obot  
**Status:** Conceptual research and falsifiable architectural proposals

> **Universal interface ≠ universal internal architecture.**
>
> An AI system may understand a task through natural language without natural language being the most reliable, efficient, governable, or authorized representation in which to perform it.

Welcome to **Beyond the LLM Boundary (BLB-SHI)**. This guide explains the research question, the eight-paper reading sequence, and how to evaluate the proposed **Boundary-Aware Structural Hybrid Intelligence (BASHI)** architecture without mistaking hypotheses for experimental results.

---

## 1. The Research Question in One Minute

LLMs provide a remarkable general interface to knowledge, reasoning, and software tools. Yet broad linguistic competence alone does not establish that one representation, model, execution strategy, ownership arrangement, or learning mechanism is best for every task.

BLB-SHI asks:

**When should intelligence remain inside a general-purpose language model, and when should a system locate, invoke, verify, and govern a different computational structure?**

The proposed answer is **BASHI**, an architecture that combines:

- **Boundary diagnosis** across representation, computation, learning, perspective, ownership, and governance.
- **Structural localization** to identify the relevant context, node, capability, or owner.
- **Hybrid execution** through an LLM, specialized model, graph, solver, tool, or other authorized mechanism.
- **Evidence and counter-evidence** to assess outputs and structural changes.
- **Policy control** that enforces permissions and ownership across the entire process.
- **Evidence-gated structural growth** rather than uncontrolled accumulation of components.

This is a **research program**, not a claim of an implemented or benchmark-validated system.

```text
  TASK + GOALS + CONTEXT + PERSPECTIVE + POLICY
                         |
                 Boundary Diagnosis
      (representation / computation / learning /
         perspective / ownership / governance)
                         |
                Structural Localization
              (MDT / CCC / UTN / graphs)
                         |
               Authorized Route Selection
                         |
                  Hybrid Execution
       (LLM / specialized model / solver / tool)
                         |
             Verification + Counter-Evidence
                         |
              Result / Audit / Explanation
                         |
          Candidate Structural Delta Growth
                  (test -> review ->
                 authorize -> promote)

   POLICY CONTROL PLANE applies across every stage.
   No score or performance gain overrides authorization.
```

**Localization asks:** *Where is the relevant intelligence, structure, context, or owner?*  
**Execution asks:** *Which computational mechanism should act there?*  
**Governance asks:** *Who authorizes the action and any subsequent learning?*

These are related but not interchangeable decisions.

## 6. How to Read the Claims Critically

The series distinguishes four kinds of statements:

1. **Established background:** documented properties of existing LLMs, formal tools, and hybrid systems, subject to cited evidence.
2. **Conceptual distinction:** a definition or analytic separation, such as descriptive versus computational expressibility.
3. **Architecture proposal:** a possible design, contract, or routing procedure that still requires implementation.
4. **Empirical hypothesis:** a prediction that needs controlled measurement and may be falsified.

In particular:

- The illustrative percentages sometimes used to discuss language-accessible intelligence are **not measured population-level estimates**.
- The existence of a representation barrier does **not** imply that an LLM cannot encode or use that representation.
- A larger LLM may outperform a specialized or hybrid system for many tasks; hybridization introduces routing, integration, security, and maintenance costs.
- Centralized systems can implement privacy and permission controls; perspective sovereignty is an architectural and governance question, not proof that all-in-one models necessarily violate ownership.
- CCC-native computation, open-ended growth, and BASHI's overall performance remain **unvalidated research directions**.

A strong evaluation must include competitive **LLM-only**, **LLM-with-tools**, and **hybrid-system** baselines, with comparable quality, latency, cost, reliability, and authorization constraints.

## 7. Suggested Evaluation Checklist

Before claiming that BASHI or any component improves a task, ask:

- **Task:** Is the workload defined and representative, including difficult counterexamples?
- **Baseline:** Is a strong tool-using LLM included, not just a weak language-only model?
- **Representation:** Is translation fidelity measured separately from downstream task success?
- **Economics:** Are routing, integration, maintenance, verification, and governance costs counted?
- **Ownership:** Are context boundaries and authorization requirements enforced and tested?
- **Verification:** Is evidence quality assessed independently of model confidence?
- **Growth:** Are structural updates sandboxed, reviewable, reversible, and evaluated for regressions?
- **Falsification:** What result would demonstrate that the proposed structural approach is not advantageous?

A negative or mixed result is valuable: the objective is to discover **where** each architectural strategy works, not to force a universal winner.
